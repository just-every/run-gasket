# Gasket product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Gasket is a complete mobile CMMS for US maintenance teams replacing MaintainX, UpKeep or Fiix by Rockwell Automation and competing with Limble and Tractian. It keeps equipment available by turning faults, preventive requirements and live condition evidence into timely, resourced work, then preserving the technician evidence, spare-parts movements, purchasing, maintenance costs and reliability outcomes. It serves the full owned-equipment maintenance job, from one workshop to multiple plants and storerooms.

This is the product we are building towards. Missing implementation is engineering work, never a reduction of this destination. Every named clause states buyer behavior and acceptance through a public page, form, mobile app, JSON API, command, download or provider/gateway door. [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) repeats every journey and its current status. The final Status section judges implementation at a pinned commit; local flow tests do not prove a hosted rollout, real vendor extraction, native app distribution or physical equipment safety.

## Product and authority rules

An owner installation starts empty. Demonstration fixtures establish no live capability. The initial site, asset register, inspections, parts movements, independent review and agent-proposal guarantees below remain part of the full product; the prior single-site, Australian, manual-only and no-cost/no-offline/no-notification limits describe current gaps rather than target boundaries.

Every private handler authorizes current identity, role, site and record, including direct API calls, downloads and offline sync. Required evidence and approvals are server rules. A contributor cannot approve their own work, an agent cannot self-approve, and a purchase initiator cannot authorize their own spend. Source records, units, timestamps, corrections and human decisions remain attributable. Automation is installation-owned, restart-safe, visible and stoppable; neither retries nor migration may create duplicate work, stock, bills or notices.

The scheduler's existing fixed-calendar and approval-reading meter cadence remains GAS-05's supported mode; PM-003 adds explicit buyer-selectable policies. Existing three-decimal quantities remain exact. Current 1.5 MB work evidence storage is an implementation limit to replace with declared target storage under ADMIN-008 and SAFE-003, not permission to discard source attachments.

## Published maintenance guarantees

- **GAS-01 — Set up the site and its people.** The owner names the initial site and time zone, invites people as lead, maintainer, supervisor or agent, changes roles and ends access. Every JSON, form, document and download handler enforces the current session and role; ending access revokes existing sessions. New owner installations start empty. ADMIN-014 extends invitation and sign-in to people identified only by a mobile phone number, and ADMIN-015 adds Sign in with Google. **Acceptance evidence:** Through site and access pages and JSON/form doors, configure a site, invite and sign in each role, change one role and end access. Repeat private reads and writes signed out, forbidden and revoked; require refusal without leaked records.

- **GAS-02 — Keep the asset register.** A lead records asset tag, name, serial, location, custodian, category, criticality and counter, gauge or condition meters with units. Edits retain earlier values. Sightings record found, elsewhere or missing; retirement preserves history and prevents new scheduled work. **Acceptance evidence:** Create and edit an asset through the register, add three meter types, record each sighting outcome and retire another asset. Read its previous identity and history; duplicate tags, stale edits and unauthorized changes are refused.

- **GAS-03 — Keep the parts list and reconcile counts.** A lead keeps part numbers, names, units, stock locations and minimum quantities, receives stock and records physical counts. Counts post exact variance movements without rewriting receipts. Quantities are exact to three decimals; stale counts and invalid precision are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Receive five units through the parts page, count four against the observed version, inspect the minus-one variance and retained receipt, then submit a stale count and excess precision through the API; neither changes stock.

- **GAS-04 — Write checklists with revisions.** Leads create checklists and publish revisions. Issued work orders keep the exact procedure revision with which they were generated; editing the master never changes issued instructions or earlier results. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a checklist and issue a PM, publish a revision with an extra step, then open the issued work and revision history. Existing work retains its old steps and later work uses the new revision.

- **GAS-05 — Schedule preventive work.** Leads define calendar intervals, counter intervals or both, first due date/value and default maintainer. The explicit Create due work orders command generates work due within seven days or at the counter threshold, with at most one open order per schedule. Automatic operation extends this guarantee in PM-001 and PM-002. **Acceptance evidence:** Create calendar and meter schedules through the API, cross each due boundary and request generation twice. Inspect one linked order per due schedule, the retained due value and no duplicate open work.

- **GAS-06 — Assign work.** Leads create corrective work against an asset and assign or reassign work to active maintainers, leads or the owner; technicians also create and self-assign their own unplanned work under FIELD-009. Maintainers have their own work list; assigning a supervisor, agent, ended member or unknown person is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Raise corrective work through the form door, assign and reassign it, then read both maintainers' own queues. Attempt each ineligible assignee through the JSON door and inspect unchanged assignment.

- **GAS-07 — Capture the inspection.** The assigned maintainer records pass, fail or needs-attention results with notes, uploads photo or document evidence and records dated meter or condition readings. Other people cannot record the work. Backwards counter readings and future readings are refused; evidence downloads require access. **Acceptance evidence:** As the assigned technician, enter all result types, upload and download evidence, and record readings through work pages/API. Try another technician, a backwards counter and a future date; require refusal and retained original evidence.

- **GAS-08 — Issue and return parts, record downtime and findings.** Assigned maintainers issue available parts, return no more than their holdings, record outage start and end with computed minutes, and record severity-coded findings. One asset has at most one open downtime. A finding requesting follow-up creates a linked corrective work order. **Acceptance evidence:** Issue and return parts through the work form/API, attempt excess stock and returns, open and close downtime, then request corrective follow-up from a finding. Inspect exact stock, minutes, linked work and refusal of overlapping open downtime.

- **GAS-09 — Submit and review.** Submission requires all checklist results, a corrective finding linked to every failed or needs-attention result and closed downtime. Supervisors approve the exact reviewed version or return it with a reason. A person who performed work cannot approve it; stale review is refused. Approval closes work and advances the schedule. **Acceptance evidence:** Submit two failed steps with only one finding and require refusal, then supply linked findings and complete inspection evidence. Return with a reason, resubmit, attempt self-review and stale approval, and approve independently. Inspect retained reviews, closure and next schedule due point.

- **GAS-10 — Watch overdue work and downtime.** Leads and supervisors see overdue work, work awaiting review, unresolved corrective follow-ups, assets down now, downtime minutes per asset over the last 30 days and stock below minimum. Open downtime contributes elapsed time; historic outages are clipped to the reporting window. **Acceptance evidence:** Open the board as lead and supervisor with overdue, submitted, failed and down assets plus low stock. Compare counts and clipped minutes with source records; a maintainer cannot read the board API.

- **GAS-11 — Read and keep the history.** Members read asset work, results, readings, part movements, downtime, findings and reviews with actors and dates; leads and supervisors download the same history as CSV. The owner exports the whole installation with rer export and restores it into a fresh installation with rer import without losing domain history. **Acceptance evidence:** Read the full asset history and authorized CSV through public doors, compare events and attribution, export a populated installation using rer export, restore into an empty installation using rer import and compare domain records and attachment digests.

- **GAS-12 — Move in from MaintainX.** The existing register importer accepts MaintainX asset, part and meter-reading CSV columns. A no-write preview lists creates, rejected rows and attention items; apply is bound to the reviewed fingerprint, journaled and idempotent. Undo removes only that import and refuses when later records depend on it. MIG-002 extends this to complete work history. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload the three CSVs through move-in, compare a no-write preview, refuse a changed fingerprint, apply and reapply, undo and import again. Create dependent Gasket records and verify undo reports blockers without removing data.

- **GAS-13 — Let an agent prepare and a person approve.** An agent uses its own sign-in and the documented JSON API to read maintenance data and propose a schedule, corrective work or physical count. Proposals retain reasoning, payload hash and relied-on versions. A different lead or owner approves or rejects; approval executes the normal command atomically. Stale proposals and direct agent mutations are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in an agent, prepare each supported proposal via API, reject one, approve another as a different lead, change underlying stock and attempt stale approval. Try direct work capture, record mutation and self-approval; inspect refusals and attributed decisions.

- **HTTP-001 — Health, Lobby and real 404s.** The installed origin serves the Lobby at /, deployment health and identity at /health, and real 404s for unknown HTTP and document routes. Private maintenance pages remain behind native sign-in. **Acceptance evidence:** Request the emitted Worker's root, health, unknown document, unknown API and health subpath; inspect branding, deployment identity and HTTP status, then attempt a private page signed out.

## Target clauses

### Administration, trust and ownership

- **ADMIN-001 — US operating defaults.** A US owner configures USD costs, US English as the organization's default display language (ADMIN-013 lets each person choose Spanish or another language), a US postal address and site time zones with daylight saving. Assets support customary and SI units with explicit conversions; schedules and reports show site-local dates and unambiguous timestamps. Existing data keeps its recorded units and currency. **Acceptance evidence:** Set up Ohio and Arizona sites, enter USD hourly rates and Fahrenheit/psi readings, cross a daylight-saving boundary and export. Verify due times, currency, unit labels and unchanged original readings.

- **ADMIN-002 — Multiple sites and shared governance.** One organization manages sites, buildings, lines and storerooms with local calendars and shared asset/part standards. Owners delegate site managers; site membership scopes every list, attachment, report and write. Cross-site transfers retain identity and history. Reparenting a hierarchy entry (ASSET-001) under another site's parent needs management rights at both sites and is otherwise refused. The technician create-work right (FIELD-009) is turned off and on per site by its owner or site manager: that site's technicians are refused at every door while other sites' technicians still create work. The stores report (PART-009) filters by any site the reader may see. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two sites with distinct managers, move an asset with its meter history, then call each private door using the other site's member. Inspect denied access, correct local dates and organization rollups. As a manager of one site only, move a machine under the other site's line and see it refused (ASSET-001). Turn the technician create-work right off at one site and see that site's technicians refused at the web, API and app doors while the other site's technicians still create work (FIELD-009).

- **ADMIN-003 — Role rights and separation of duties.** Owner controls identity, integrations and retention; site admin manages that site; lead plans/assigns and manages registers; technician creates corrective and emergency work, including work already completed, against assets and locations (FIELD-009) and records assigned or self-assigned work; supervisor reviews independently; storeroom clerk counts/issues/receives, and a stores supervisor independently approves count variances (PART-004); buyer drafts POs; purchasing approver authorizes spend; analyst reads authorized reports. Permission templates and custom grants keep work creation, execution, approval and cost-rate visibility separate. Create work is a default technician right that an owner or site admin revokes per role and site, and a ready-made Operator template sees and records only work assigned to that person. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in each role and exercise its permitted public doors, including direct forbidden writes/exports. Add a custom site-scoped role, revoke it and test old sessions; technician work and purchasing cannot be self-approved. Remove create work from the technician role at one site and see that site's technicians refused at app and API while the other site's technicians still create work; give a person the Operator template and see only their assigned work.

- **ADMIN-004 — Requester and contractor access.** Requesters and contractors are invited by mobile phone number or email address (ADMIN-014); neither needs a company email. Requesters submit and follow their own faults without seeing private maintenance costs or other people's requests. Contractors receive expiring access to named work, instructions and evidence, record completion and costs, and cannot browse the asset estate or approve their work. Neither ever reads the team's internal comments or their photos (PLAN-006). Requesters and contractors never create work orders at any door (FIELD-009); they use request intake (REQ-001, REQ-002). **Acceptance evidence:** Invite a requester by US mobile number only and an external electrician by email, have the requester sign in with a one-time SMS code, submit a fault and follow it, and finish assigned contractor work. Change URL/API identifiers to other records, inspect denied access, expire the contractor grant and test its active session. The requester and the contractor are refused a work order's and an asset's internal comments and comment photos (PLAN-006) by URL and API. A requester and a contractor are refused work creation at the web, API and app doors (FIELD-009).

- **ADMIN-005 — Enterprise identity lifecycle.** Microsoft Entra ID and Okta SAML/OIDC SSO, enforced MFA policy and SCIM provisioning/deprovisioning map organization groups to site roles. Owner recovery is audited; deprovisioning ends browser, API and mobile access, including queued offline writes at sync. The same applies to people who sign in by phone number (ADMIN-014): ending a phone-only person's access, or changing their number, ends their app sessions and offline sync. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect each identity provider at its network edge, provision two site groups, require MFA, deprovision an active technician and attempt web/API/offline sync. Repeat for a phone-only technician with queued offline work: end their access and attempt app sync. Inspect refusal of every attempt and a logged owner recovery.

- **ADMIN-006 — Attributed append-only audit.** Maintenance changes, immutable readings and movements, checklist revisions, reviews and import decisions commit atomically with actor, action, record and time. Owners and authorized supervisors search/export audit by site, action and date, including integration and AI decisions; corrections append rather than rewrite evidence. While an installation holds one site (until ADMIN-002), the site filter of audit search and export accepts that one site and refuses any other with 404, and every entry and CSV row names its site; per-site attribution across several sites grows with ADMIN-002. **Acceptance evidence:** Perform work, count, review and import through public doors, trigger a stale write, and inspect the audit download. The failed write adds no business change; previous readings remain visible and integration/AI actions name their authority. Filter search and export by the installation's site and by another site id (404); every entry and CSV row names its site.

- **ADMIN-007 — Retention and verifiable erasure.** Owners set retention and legal holds for attachments, request contacts, users and maintenance records. An erasure preview identifies dependencies and retained obligations; apply removes eligible records/files, anonymizes retained audit, and produces a per-type result. Later restores honor the erasure journal; recipients' prior downloads remain outside local erasure. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a held work record and an eligible requester/photo, preview and apply erasure through the owner door, inspect bytes and anonymized audit, and restore an older archive with the later erasure journal. Held records remain and erased records do not reappear.

- **ADMIN-008 — Protected credentials and private attachments.** Provider tokens, sensor certificates and mobile credentials are encrypted under installation authority, narrowly scoped, rotatable and revocable. Private file access rechecks current record rights; original attachments use Cloudflare R2 or owner object storage with resumable uploads, declared limits, malware handling and safe rendering. Telemetry aggregation runs in bounded background jobs rather than long request-time database scans. Logs, exports and AI context redact secrets; disconnecting a provider stops new sends. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect/rotate/revoke a provider, upload a private manual and hostile file, then use unauthorized, expired and cross-site download links. Export and inspect logs/context for planted secrets; revoked connectors produce no new outbound requests.

- **ADMIN-009 — Complete portable archive and inactive restore.** Owners download a documented archive of all sites, assets, work/history, PMs, requests, stock/movements, POs, costs, attachments, audit and import mappings with schema versions, counts and digests. Restore into empty data validates before writes, pauses automation and excludes sessions/provider secrets; owners reconnect services deliberately. **Acceptance evidence:** Export a realistic multi-site estate through the supported command/download, interrupt/resume, validate digests and restore to a fresh installation. Compare every record class; corrupt and nonempty restores write nothing and restored PMs do not send or buy.

- **ADMIN-010 — Backup recovery and connector operations.** The owner sees backup freshness, last successful restore drill, background-worker and connector lag, failed jobs, quotas and actionable failures. Recovery preserves accepted work and notification/purchasing idempotency; an operator can pause a failing automation without deleting its history. **Acceptance evidence:** Back up populated data, interrupt a PM/receipt job, recover to another installation and reconnect intentionally. Inspect visible recovery point, job errors and deduplicated resumed work through owner operations pages.

- **ADMIN-011 — Accessible responsive maintenance workspace.** Core setup, intake, planning, stores, reporting and work execution are usable at 1440 and 390 widths with keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels, visible focus and text status. Long asset names and errors remain readable; failed saves keep entered work and disclose whether it was persisted. **Acceptance evidence:** Use every core journey at 1440 and 390 with realistic long records, keyboard and assistive semantics. Induce a save failure, reload the confirmed record and inspect retained draft, focus, text errors and absence of horizontal clipping.

- **ADMIN-012 — Search, saved views and bulk maintenance actions.** People search/filter assets, work, requests, procedures, parts and vendors by tag, serial, site, owner, date, status and custom fields. Saved views and permission-aware bulk edit/assign/export show a preview and per-record failures; pagination never silently omits the estate. **Acceptance evidence:** Find a serial among thousands of assets, save a cross-filtered view, bulk reassign selected work including a forbidden record and export all pages. Inspect exact selection, denial and totals.

- **ADMIN-013 — Per-person display language.** Owners set the organization's default display language and every person (owner, lead, technician, supervisor, stores, purchasing, requester, contractor) chooses their own. US English and US Spanish ship complete at launch; the published catalog covers at least 30 display languages, including French, Portuguese, Vietnamese, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Korean, Tagalog, Polish and right-to-left Arabic. The chosen language applies to the web workspace, the iOS and Android app (FIELD-001), the requester portal and QR fault form (REQ-001, REQ-002), email and push notices (PM-005, INT-004), printed work packs (FIELD-008) and PDF reports (REPORT-006). Signed-out requesters get the portal's site default or their browser preference and can switch. Dates and numbers follow the person's locale while stored values, units, USD amounts and timestamps are unchanged. User-entered text (asset names, checklist steps, notes, readings, comments) is shown exactly as authored, and translated procedures come only from AGENT-007's reviewed translations. A missing translation shows the English string with a visible untranslated marker, never a blank control, and owners can download a per-language completeness report. **Acceptance evidence:** Set the organization default to English and one technician and one requester to Spanish. On the technician's phone app, the requester's QR fault form and portal, an emailed and a push notification, and a printed work pack, show interface text in Spanish while user-entered checklist text, readings, units, USD amounts and audit attribution stay byte-for-byte unchanged and an English lead still sees English. Remove one Spanish catalog string and show the visible English fallback marker, not a blank control, plus the completeness report naming it. Switch a user to Arabic and check right-to-left layout at 390 width without clipping.

- **ADMIN-014 — Phone-number invites and SMS sign-in.** An owner, site admin or lead invites any person (technician, supervisor, storeroom clerk, buyer, requester or contractor) by mobile phone number or by email address; neither is required. A phone invite is a text message, sent through the owner's connected SMS provider (Twilio or Amazon SNS), with an expiring single-use acceptance link. The person then signs in to the web workspace and the iOS and Android app (FIELD-001) with a six-digit one-time code sent by SMS and valid for 10 minutes. Numbers are stored in E.164 form and identify one person in the organization. Duplicate numbers, malformed numbers and numbers in countries the owner has not enabled (the US and Canada at launch) are refused with a visible reason. A number that has opted out by replying STOP is shown as unreachable rather than silently retried. Code requests are limited to five per number in 15 minutes and per network address, five wrong codes lock the number for 15 minutes, and codes never appear in logs, exports or AI context. A person can add an email address later. Changing a number requires a code sent to the new number. Changing the number or ending access revokes every existing web and app session, API token and device grant, and the person's queued offline writes are refused at sync with a per-record reason that stays visible to them and their lead (FIELD-003). Invitations, code requests, sign-ins, number changes and opt-outs appear in the audit (ADMIN-006). SMS delivery status is visible on the invitation, and provider acceptance never counts as acceptance by the person. Imported users with only a phone number become phone-identified invitations mapped to their history (MIG-002, MIG-008). **Acceptance evidence:** (1) Invite a technician by US mobile number only and inspect the SMS invitation at the provider edge. (2) Accept it, sign in to the iOS and Android app with the one-time SMS code, and receive and complete assigned work. (3) Invite a requester the same way and follow their own request. (4) Import a MaintainX user export containing a phone-only user and see that user mapped to their completed work. (5) Submit a duplicate number, a malformed number and a number from a country that is not enabled, and see each refused with a visible reason. (6) Request a sixth code within 15 minutes and see it refused with a retry time, then enter five wrong codes and see the lockout. (7) Change one phone-only technician's number and end another's access while both hold queued offline work, then sync: existing sessions are revoked, every queued write is refused with a visible reason, and the audit records each event.

- **ADMIN-015 — Sign in with Google.** Owners enable Sign in with Google for the organization, optionally limited to named Google Workspace domains, alongside email-link, SMS (ADMIN-014) and enterprise SSO (ADMIN-005) sign-in. A Google account signs in only when its verified email matches an invited or active member, unless the owner sets a domain rule that admits new members at a named default role for review. Members can link and unlink Google from their profile, and owners can require Google or SSO sign-in for owners and leads. Ending access in Gasket revokes existing sessions regardless of the Google session, and the provider credentials are protected under ADMIN-008. **Acceptance evidence:** Enable Google with test credentials at the network edge, sign in an invited lead with Google and land on their own work, and see an uninvited Google account and an account outside the allowed Workspace domain refused. Unlink Google and sign in by email link, require Google for leads and see an email-link lead sign-in refused, then end the lead's access and see their Google sign-in refused and their active session revoked.

### Assets, procedures and equipment context

- **ASSET-001 — Asset and location hierarchy.** Leads model site/building/line/machine/component parentage with stable identifiers and locations. Moving a subtree preserves work and rolls cost/downtime up without counting an outage twice; cycles and cross-site unauthorized reparenting are refused. Exactly: the site is the root; under it every register entry is a building, line, machine or component, and keeps its id and tag through every move. A parent ranks above its child in that order (a component may also hold components), is active, and is never the entry itself or anything below it; a stale read, a parent of the wrong level, a retired parent and a cycle are refused and write nothing, and an entry with active entries below it cannot be retired. Leads (and the owner) place an entry when they register it and move it later; moving an entry takes everything below it along, work, downtime, readings and history stay on the entries they were recorded on, and the move shows in the history of the entry and of both parents. An entry's roll-up covers it and everything below it: open work, ended downtime as the union of their outages so an outage they share counts once (the shared minutes are shown; entries down now are listed and their outage counts once it ends, as on each asset), and moved-in work cost (MIG-002) in parts, labor and other. Done covers the one site and the cost records that exist today; ADMIN-002 refuses reparenting across sites without rights at both, REPORT-001 rolls its work costs up this hierarchy, and MIG-001 places imported assets under the parents their source names, each before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a compressor under a line, move its component subtree and open parent history/report totals. Attempt a cycle and cross-site move; verify refused writes and retained descendant records.

- **ASSET-002 — QR and barcode identification.** Leads print durable QR and barcode labels for assets, locations and bins. Scanning with a phone camera or supported scanner opens the authorized record, the scoped fault form (REQ-002) or, for a member with the create-work right, new work against that asset or location (FIELD-009); a public label never grants private work/history access. Reprints preserve identity. **Acceptance evidence:** Print asset and location labels, scan at a technician phone and signed-out operator portal, report a fault against a location label with no asset, reprint a retired asset label and scan an unknown code. Inspect correct route, restricted fields and explicit retired/unknown states.

- **ASSET-003 — Manuals, documents and warranties.** Assets hold versioned OEM manuals, diagrams, warranties, supplier contacts, commissioning dates and authorized documents with expiry reminders. Technicians see the version linked to issued work; offline packs include approved files and retain original attribution. A document is a manual, diagram, warranty, authorization (permit, licence or certificate) or other document of one asset, kept as numbered revisions of a PDF, image or plain-text file of up to 1.5 MB that never change or go away; each revision names its source (publisher and reference) and who added it when. Leads (and the owner) add documents, revisions and contacts; adding a revision, from the revision the lead read (a stale one is refused), is its approval and makes it the master. A lead links a document to open work on the same asset, pinning the current revision or a named earlier one; the work shows and packs that revision, marked when the master has moved on, until a lead links it again. The job pack is one ZIP download of the work order's summary, the pinned files unchanged and a manifest of each file's document, revision, original name, source, SHA-256 and who added it; FIELD-002's offline packs inherit the same pinned files and attribution before they ship. An expiring document names an active member as its owner and reminder days (0 to 365, default 30): on a scheduler run (PM-001) the owner gets one in-app notice once the site's day is within that many days before the expiry date and one more on or after it, and a changed date asks again; email and push wait for PM-005. Supplier, manufacturer and service contacts, each with a phone or email, are kept on the asset; commissioning dates are ASSET-007's lifecycle facts. **Acceptance evidence:** Attach two manual revisions, link one to work, set a warranty expiration and download the job pack. Change the master and inspect issued/offline documents plus the named owner's expiry reminder.

- **ASSET-004 — Status, criticality and failure taxonomy.** Asset states distinguish available, degraded, down, out of service and retired; criticality, risk, failure mode, cause and remedy codes are maintained centrally and mapped on imports. A status change keeps its effective time and reason; manual and telemetry disagreement is visible. The catalog is one site-wide list per kind: criticality is an ordered list of levels (seeded Normal, Important, Critical) that leads add to, reorder, relabel and retire, and risk, failure mode, cause and remedy codes are maintained the same way. A code's key never changes; every record keeps the code it was given, a retired code stays readable on it and is no longer given to new records or matched by imports. An imported value maps onto the active code whose key, label or one of its import names equals it, ignoring case; a value that matches none is refused for criticality and moves in unclassified, with an attention item, for a failure code. Findings carry optional failure mode, cause, remedy and risk codes, and the failure report counts findings and moved-in work by code, lists what is unclassified and names the assets whose evidence disagrees with their declared state. Done covers every importer that exists today (MaintainX criticality and work categories); each later importer (MIG-001 through MIG-008) maps the codes its source carries this way before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Take a critical pump down, correct its outage classification and map an imported failure code. Inspect effective status history and unclassified/contradictory evidence in reports without rewriting the original event.

- **ASSET-005 — Meters, units and counter corrections.** Meters record source time, receipt time, unit, quality and provenance for counter/gauge/condition readings. Reset, replacement, rollover and corrected readings require an explained append-only adjustment; stale or suspect readings cannot silently fire PMs or reverse counters. Staleness is per meter feed: each meter declares a feed window in whole hours (1 to 168, 24 by default; a daily-cadence feed can declare 36), and a feed reading received more than that window after its source time is stale. Manual readings, attested by the person who took them on a work order, are not aged. A reading keeps the quality it was received with when the window later changes, and creating due work lists the schedules that stale or suspect readings hold back. **Acceptance evidence:** Enter manual readings then a replacement counter and late/corrected feed through public doors. Compare normalized run hours, quality flags and due work, and attempt an unexplained backwards reading.

- **ASSET-006 — Bill of materials and interchangeable spares.** Each asset/component has a revisioned bill of materials, required quantities, approved substitutes, compatible serial/model ranges and linked tools. Work planning sees stock at eligible storerooms and warns when a substitute is not approved. Exactly: a lead or owner saves an asset's bill of materials as numbered revisions, never rewriting an earlier one (a stale revision number is refused): lines of parts with the quantity a repair needs and an optional serial range the line fits, approved alternate parts per line each with its own optional serial range, and the tools the work needs, named. Serial ranges include both ends and compare in natural order (digit runs as numbers, letters ignoring case); an asset without a serial fits only lines and alternates without a range. The asset page and every work order on the asset show the current revision with each part's and alternate's available stock (PART-002), whether it fits the asset's serial and which lines are short, and earlier revisions stay listed. Reserving (PART-002) accepts an alternate the current revision lists for that line only when it fits the asset's serial, otherwise a site-wide approved substitute, and refuses with a visible reason a part or alternate outside its serial range or a substitute neither approves; that refusal is the warning. Done covers what exists today: one storeroom per site, assets without component parentage, tools as names and serial numbers as the only compatibility attribute; ASSET-001 gives components their own revisions, PART-001 shows stock per eligible storeroom, PLAN-004 links the named tools to its tool register and ASSET-009 adds model ranges once a model field exists, each before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a motor BOM with two approved bearings and a serial restriction, plan a repair with one out of stock and select a substitute. Inspect compatibility and quantities on the resulting work/parts reservation.

- **ASSET-007 — Asset lifecycle and replacement decision.** Leads record purchase, commissioning, warranty, service agreement, replacement value and retirement/disposal with retained service history. A replacement comparison uses actual maintenance cost and downtime; accounting depreciation remains in the connected ledger. Each lifecycle change is a numbered revision (a stale one is refused) recorded in the asset's history, and a retired asset's facts no longer change. Retiring records the disposal (sold, scrapped, returned, transferred, donated or other, its date and optional proceeds). The comparison's repair cost is the sum of every cost entry (REPORT-005) on the asset's own work orders in any status, unknown (never zero) when there are none; its downtime is the minutes of the asset's ended failure stops by their latest classification (REPORT-003); the repair cost is shown as a percentage of the replacement value to one decimal. - **ASSET-008 — Inspections, rounds and calibration.** Leads publish multi-asset inspection routes and calibration tasks with due tolerances, required tools, certificate attachments and failed-reading follow-up. Each stop records its asset and procedure revision; missed or failed stops stay open independently. A route is an ordered list of at least two stops, each an asset and the checklist done there; issuing it makes a run with one work order per stop, all due the same day and pinned to each checklist's current revision, so a failed reading (FIELD-004) raises its follow-up on that stop alone. A missed stop keeps its reason and stays open; a supervisor (the review right) signs a run off only once every stop is closed, so signing never closes missing work. A route may repeat every N whole days from its first due date (fixed cadence, to a default person): the scheduler (PM-001) and Create due work orders issue one run per occurrence (`route@date`) once its date is within the site's lead time, whatever state earlier runs are in, and a route with a retired stop waits. A route or schedule may name required tools; each work order it issues lists them, and its submission waits until the person doing it records which one was used (a serial or asset number). A calibration task is a schedule on an instrument whose procedure carries the tolerances: its number steps with inclusive limits (FIELD-004), at least one required, so a reading outside them fails and needs a linked finding with follow-up work. Its due tolerance is a grace window of 0 to 90 whole days after the due date, in the site's time zone: the task reads in date before the due date, due through due date + grace, overdue after; its cadence runs from the day its work is approved unless the lead chooses fixed (PM-003). Its work needs a certificate (an attached PDF or image marked as the certificate) before review; approval keeps the latest certificate as the instrument's next certificate version with the procedure revision, the tools used, whether it was on time (approved by due date + grace) and its result: out of tolerance when any number step failed, in tolerance otherwise. That result is Gasket's reading of the recorded values against the procedure's limits; Gasket does not certify the instrument or verify the reference standard's own certification. Earlier versions are kept. A tool register with its own calibration due dates and booking is PLAN-004's, and compliance template libraries, qualification links and audit packs are SAFE-002's, each owed to calibration tasks before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Schedule a route across three assets and a gauge calibration, fail one reading and skip one stop. ### Preventive maintenance and automatic follow-up

- **PM-001 — Unattended calendar generation.** An installation-owned scheduler generates recurring calendar PMs without a person pressing a button, using each site's time zone, lead time and fixed- or completion-based policy. Each due occurrence has a stable identity; restart, overlap and retry create one work order and retain missed-run debt. The scheduler is a Cron Trigger every 15 minutes (UTC), declared for the deployed Worker and run by `rer dev`; each run issues, at the site's time zone, calendar work the site's lead time ahead of its due date (whole days, 0 to 90, 7 by default; the owner sets it on the Site page or through `site.configure`) and counter or trigger work as soon as it is due (PM-002, PM-003). Manual generation uses the same lead time. Gasket keeps one site per installation today, so each installation's site has its own lead time; ADMIN-002 inherits a lead time per site before it ships. An occurrence is its schedule plus its due date (or counter value, or triggering reading); the scheduler never issues an occurrence that already had a work order, so a canceled occurrence stays owed until a person reissues or skips it. Every run is kept; a run more than 45 minutes after the one before records the gap, and calendar work the scheduler issued after its due date is listed as issued late, on the Schedules page and in `scheduler`. **Acceptance evidence:** Save a monthly PM through the schedule door, leave the app, cross its due window and restart the scheduler twice. Open one generated order, its due occurrence and any missed-run warning without invoking manual generation.

- **PM-002 — Automatic meter and condition triggers.** Validated manual or integrated counter, gauge and condition readings automatically evaluate thresholds, trend duration and hysteresis. Combined calendar/meter policies state whichever-first or both; repeated/late sensor deliveries do not duplicate work or satisfy a condition with stale data. Every reading a door keeps (a feed reading or one taken on a work order) evaluates the schedules on its meter at once. A gauge trigger fires when good readings stay strictly above (or below) its threshold and a condition trigger when they stay in its chosen conditions, for at least its sustain minutes measured between source times (0: the first such reading); after firing it re-arms only once a later good reading is at or past its reset value (the threshold unless set; for a condition, out of the chosen conditions). Only good readings received after the trigger was set up count, so stale, suspect, moved-in or earlier readings never fire one. A counter is due at its value. A schedule with a calendar and a meter is due on whichever comes first unless it states both. A feed delivery repeating a kept reading (same meter, feed, source time and value) is that reading, not a new one, and a schedule never has more than one open order. Generated work names the reading that raised it. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure counter and sustained-temperature triggers, send fresh, repeated, late and recovered readings through ingest and await generated work. Inspect one order per trigger, source evidence and explicit rejection of stale data.

- **PM-003 — Cadence, seasonal windows and exceptions.** Leads choose fixed versus completion cadence, seasonal months, weekdays, holidays, shutdown windows and bounded deferral with a reason. Skipping a task retains the missed obligation and independent risk approval; editing schedules previews already-issued and future work. Due dates are site-local calendar days, so a daylight-saving change never moves one. A calendar schedule may allow only some months and ISO weekdays and, unless it is set to run through them, avoids the site's closures (holidays and shutdown windows, both ends inclusive, kept by leads); a due date on a day it may not fall moves forward to the next allowed day, never earlier. Fixed cadence counts from the planned (nominal) date, so moves never drift it; completion cadence counts from the day the work is approved. A lead asks to skip an overdue occurrence that has no open work, or to defer the current occurrence to a date after it and at most the schedule's deferral limit later (14 days unless set, up to 365), always with a reason; nothing moves until an owner or supervisor other than the person who asked approves it (on a site with one owner and no supervisor, that owner may approve their own request after an explicit second confirmation, and the decision is labeled single-approver), issued work moves with an approved deferral and keeps its checklist revision, and every request and decision stays on record, so a skipped occurrence remains recorded as missed. Editing a calendar schedule's interval, cadence or calendar shows a no-write preview of its issued work (unchanged: due date and checklist revision) and of its next five due dates before and after the change. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure weekday/seasonal PMs across a holiday and daylight-saving change, defer one with approval and edit an interval. Inspect next dates, retained missed work and unchanged issued checklist/due evidence.

- **REPORT-001 — Work and asset maintenance costing.** Every work order and asset shows actual labor, consumed/returned parts, contractor, tool and miscellaneous USD costs alongside estimates, commitments and posted amounts. - **REPORT-002 — Downtime and production availability.** Report planned/unplanned outages, degraded operation, reason, failure mode, affected production window and availability denominator. Overlapping component events roll up without double counting; open outages and uncertain feed coverage remain visible with drill-down. Exactly: the report covers a period of site-local days, both ends included (the last 30 days by default), for the site (every line and every entry with an outage) or one register entry and everything below it (ASSET-001), and is read by leads, supervisors and the owner. An outage is downtime recorded on a work order; whoever records it marks it a failure stop, a planned stop or degraded operation (the asset kept running at reduced output), with its reason and an optional failure mode, and its latest classification counts (REPORT-003). Outages are clipped to the period; one still open counts to now and its entry is listed as down now. Production time is the site's declared weekly production hours (windows per ISO weekday ending by midnight, which leads and the owner add and remove; with none declared, every hour of every day), as they stand now, inside the period; the source timeline shows each outage's recorded and clipped window, its work order and line, and its minutes inside and outside production time. An entry's planned time is the production time inside a planned stop of it or of anything below it, and is left out of the availability denominator (production time less planned time). Its down time is the denominator time inside a failure stop of it or of anything below it, overlapping outages counted once with the shared minutes shown, and availability is (denominator - down time) / denominator. Degraded operation is listed on its own, does not lower availability and is not a stop (REPORT-003). Unknown coverage is denominator time that no hour meter on the entry or below it was reporting (from its first to its last good reading in the period; a stale feed reading, ASSET-005, is not good), shown in hours and never estimated or counted as up or down. Minutes are whole; hours and percentages have one decimal, rounded half up. Done covers every source of outages and production time that exists today (downtime on work orders, declared production hours and hour meters); technician after-the-fact work FIELD-009, moved-in history MIG-001 and MIG-002, machine feeds INT-010 and INT-014, and production windows from PLAN-005 and INT-017 feed these definitions before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Enter overlapping component outages across a reporting boundary and a planned shutdown, then inspect asset/line availability and source timeline. - **REPORT-003 — MTBF, MTTR and failure analysis.** Reliability reports calculate MTBF from defined operating time and qualifying failure counts, MTTR from defined repair durations, and MTTA from acknowledgment events. Definitions, sample size, exclusions and unknown inputs are displayed; failure-code Pareto and repeat failures link to work. Exactly: the report covers a period of site-local days, both ends included (the last 30 days by default), for the site or one asset, and is read by leads, supervisors and the owner. A stop is downtime recorded on a work order (degraded operation, REPORT-002, is not a stop); whoever records it marks it a planned stop or a failure, with an optional failure mode code (ASSET-004), and a lead or owner corrects that classification by appending a new one with a reason, never rewriting the stop or an earlier classification; the latest classification counts and every one stays visible with who and when. A qualifying failure is a stop that started in the period and is not classified as planned; planned stops are listed and left out of every measure. Operating time is what the asset's hour meter (a counter whose unit is h, hr, hrs, hour or hours; the first by name when there are several) counted between its first and last good reading in the period; the rest of the period is unknown coverage, shown in hours and never estimated. MTBF is operating hours divided by the failures that started inside that covered time; failures outside it, or on an asset without an hour meter, are excluded and counted. MTTR is the mean minutes from a failure's stop to back in service over failures that have ended; failures still down are excluded and counted. ### Safe maintenance records and operational limits

- **SAFE-001 — Hazard, isolation and permit evidence.** Leads attach site-approved hazard, isolation/lockout and permit procedures to work with named qualified sign-offs, prerequisites, expiry and attachments. Gasket records these human attestations and blocks administrative completion when required evidence is absent; it never determines that equipment is physically safe or energizes it. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure an isolation prerequisite, attempt work submission without the required qualified sign-off, record the permit and its expiry, and change the procedure. Inspect refusal, human attribution and required reapproval of the changed evidence.

- **SAFE-002 — Compliance templates and calibration evidence.** Organizations version maintenance/compliance templates, required inspection/calibration intervals and electronic audit packs with procedure versions, signatures, training/qualification references and retained certificates. The buyer's qualified people choose applicable standards and decide compliance; the software supplies traceable evidence without issuing certification. CSV/JSON audit manifests and original files support handoff to the buyer's separate QMS/EHS system. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a required inspection template, issue work, change the template and export an audit pack containing original instructions, results, qualifications, certificates and independent review. Verify missing/expired evidence remains explicit.

- **SAFE-003 — Capacity, ownership and honest service state.** The target supports a US organization with multiple sites, at least 10,000 assets, 100,000 parts and one million historical work orders, with paginated search/export. The supported offline pack holds at least one technician's assigned and team work for the next 7 days (no fewer than 200 work orders) with their procedures, asset histories and linked manuals, and up to 2 GB of attachments. Storage, connector and notification limits are visible before use; reaching a limit preserves accepted evidence and shows actionable failure instead of silent truncation. **Acceptance evidence:** Load a representative estate at the declared capacity through import/public API, page/search/export all records, download an offline pack at that 7-day, 200-work-order, 2 GB floor on a phone and work it in airplane mode, and exceed a configured limit. Inspect exact counts, usable core workflows and explicit refusal without lost accepted data.

## Push further

- **AGENT-001 — Detect developing failures from live evidence.** A reliability agent combines fresh sensor trends, meter quality, OEM references and repeated failure/work history to surface developing faults before breakdown. It cites exact observations, coverage, confidence and competing explanations, prepares a deduplicated corrective proposal and names an accountable lead. Lead-defined rules may authorize bounded low-risk work creation; safety-critical decisions always require qualified human review. **Acceptance evidence:** Feed authorized rising vibration, missing samples and two comparable historical failures through ingest. Inspect a source-cited proposal before the failure threshold, explicit insufficient-coverage state for gaps and no live work beyond the lead's grant.

- **AGENT-002 — Stage an executable repair around production.** The agent reads manuals, failure context, BOMs, stock, supplier lead times, technician qualifications and production windows to propose repair steps, reserve eligible parts/people/slot and chase prerequisites. A lead approves the plan/window and a purchasing approver authorizes spend; the qualified technician owns isolation and physical repair. Changed resources make the proposal stale. **Acceptance evidence:** Provide an impending motor fault with a missing bearing and a two-hour production window. Inspect a cited plan, compatible part/skill/slot choices and a purchasing draft; approve, change availability before execution and require replanning rather than silent substitution.

- **AGENT-003 — Propose evidence-backed PM interval changes.** The agent compares failures, readings, completed/late PMs, actual costs, downtime and operating exposure to propose interval changes with a cohort comparison, uncertainty and expected effect. It preserves OEM/legal/site minima as hard constraints and never edits a maintenance standard without a reliability lead's version-bound approval and rollback plan. **Acceptance evidence:** Give comparable assets different failure histories and OEM minimum intervals, inspect a cited proposed change and expected trade-off, reject an unsupported extension and approve a constrained revision. Change the source history and verify stale approval is refused.

- **AGENT-004 — Measure whether reliability actually improves.** After an approved intervention or PM change, the agent follows the affected assets across the agreed exposure window, compares downtime/failure/cost with baseline and matched peers, and reports confounders and missing coverage. It alerts the lead to worsening or unproven outcomes and proposes a reversal/new investigation; it cannot declare success from fewer logged events alone. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve a PM change, provide post-change operation with lower downtime and then a telemetry gap/production reduction. Inspect exposure-normalized results, stated uncertainty and a human-approved reversal proposal when failures worsen.

- **AGENT-005 — Follow overdue work through verified closure.** The agent watches work due dates, acknowledgments, findings, parts/access blockers and review evidence, asks the responsible person for the next action and follows promised dates. It proposes reassignment/escalation under the lead's policy and verifies linked corrective outcomes before recommending closure. Humans decide reassignment and independent supervisor approval remains mandatory. **Acceptance evidence:** Leave a PM blocked on parts and a repair awaiting review, answer one agent query with a promised date and miss it. Inspect contextual escalation, proposed reassignment and refusal to mark either work or finding closed without technician evidence and supervisor decision.

- **AGENT-006 — Reconcile spare-parts reality.** The agent compares POs, receipts, bills, work consumption, reservations and count variances to find duplicate items, missing movements, unusual demand and replenishment risk. It prepares merge/count/bin/reorder proposals with source citations and quantity/value impact. Staff physically count/receive; authorized humans approve adjustments, merges and spend, never an inferred physical receipt. **Acceptance evidence:** Provide a duplicate bearing, late PO and inconsistent count/issue history. Inspect cited correction/replenishment proposals and value impacts, approve one after a physical count, and reject an unsupported receipt. Changed stock invalidates the prepared adjustment.

- **AGENT-007 — Draft grounded procedures and technician assistance.** The assistant retrieves approved manuals, revisioned procedures, prior repairs and authorized asset history to draft checklists or answer technician questions with page/record citations and uncertainty. It also drafts translations of approved procedures, checklist steps and request-form text into a person's display language (ADMIN-013); a translated revision publishes only after a qualified bilingual reviewer approves it, stays linked to its source revision, becomes stale when the source changes, and technicians can open the original authored text beside it. Leads publish procedures after qualified review; unsafe/unsupported actions remain unresolved questions, and uploaded text cannot expand the agent's rights or operate equipment. **Acceptance evidence:** Ask for a repair checklist using an OEM manual and past work, inspect citations and conflicting instructions, then submit malicious instructions inside a manual. Publish only after lead review and verify no authority or procedure revision changes from retrieved text. Draft a Spanish translation of a lockout step, reject a mistranslation, approve a corrected version and verify its source link, then change the source and see the translation marked stale.

- **AGENT-008 — Extract purchasing documents for human review.** The agent reads supplier quote, invoice, PO or packing-slip photos/PDFs to propose part/vendor matches, pack conversions, prices, taxes and linked receipt/bill lines with highlighted source regions and confidence. Buyers resolve ambiguous matches and approve documents; the agent never asserts physical delivery or places unapproved spend. **Acceptance evidence:** Upload a skewed packing slip and invoice with a new vendor part, mismatched unit pack and tax. Inspect source-highlighted proposed matches and exceptions, correct them, approve bill creation and confirm no inventory receipt occurs without stores confirmation.

- **AGENT-009 — Accountable agent execution and measurable value.** Owners control model/provider, data scope, budget and revocable per-action grants. Every run records input versions/citations, proposed versus applied actions, cost, human decisions and measured maintenance outcome; private manuals and rates only reach authorized providers under the buyer's terms. Stop/revoke cancels pending authority and uncertain model/provider failures remain visible without fabricated results. **Acceptance evidence:** Grant a bounded reliability run with a dollar budget, inspect its data/action preview, approve one proposal and revoke during another run. Export decisions/cost/outcome history; verify no post-revocation writes, secret leakage or invented success after provider failure.

## Honest boundaries

- Gasket owns CMMS asset maintenance, operator requests, preventive and corrective work, offline execution, spare parts, maintenance purchasing and reliability reporting. Native apps, production-safe OT gateways, provider approvals, enterprise identity, original-file migration and purchasing integrations are required engineering deliverables under FIELD-001, INT-003 through INT-017, ADMIN-005 and MIG-002 through MIG-008; provider contracts or hardware do not remove those clauses.
- A qualified person performs physical maintenance, confirms receipt/counts, isolation, permits and equipment release. Gasket records procedure and human evidence under SAFE-001/SAFE-002. It does not certify safety, determine regulatory compliance, replace an emergency procedure or remotely actuate equipment; actual control remains in the PLC/SCADA or equipment system. Read-only sensor gateways and site-approved human sign-offs supply that boundary.
- Production planning, fleet dispatch, customer field-service selling, payroll (RunEditRun's [Envelope](https://github.com/just-every/run-envelope)) and bank payment execution remain separate products; rebilling completed maintenance to a customer or tenant is part of Gasket through the ledger under INT-003. Gasket exchanges production windows, fleet readings and maintenance costs with the buyer's manufacturing/fleet/ERP systems under PLAN-005 and INT-008 through INT-017. Its maintenance POs, receipts and bills stay complete; accounting/payment status comes from QuickBooks Online, SAP or Oracle under BUY-005 and the corresponding integration clauses. USD and US sales/use-tax fields are first; general-ledger tax determination and depreciation remain with the buyer's ledger/accountant.
- General accounting can be supplied by [Pantry](https://github.com/just-every/run-pantry) or the connected ledger; general customer support belongs to [HappyToHelp](https://github.com/just-every/run-happytohelp), and software/site monitoring belongs to [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime), with observability in [Porthole](https://github.com/just-every/run-porthole). Gasket's equipment fault portal and physical downtime are its own CMMS workflows. Sharing Base identity, storage or connectors never combines these products.
- Provider acceptance is distinct from human receipt; an AI suggestion is distinct from a verified diagnosis. Missing readings, incomplete migration and failed sync remain visible. Buyer-owned sensors, network access, lawful source access and the necessary provider agreements are stated setup dependencies with accountable setup/error journeys, not substitutes for a working integration.
